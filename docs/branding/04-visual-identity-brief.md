# NuBlox Visual Identity Brief

**Document status:** Draft v1.0  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Scope:** Visual identity strategy and design brief for the NuBlox master brand  
**Parent documents:** `docs/branding/00-business-entity.md`, `docs/branding/01-brand-strategy.md`, `docs/branding/02-brand-architecture-and-naming.md`, `docs/branding/03-verbal-identity-and-messaging.md`

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document defines the visual requirements for the NuBlox identity before final logo, colour, typography, iconography and design-system decisions are made.

It is intended to serve as a design brief for:

- brand designers;
- product designers;
- UI/UX designers;
- web designers;
- marketing teams;
- software-development teams;
- motion and media designers; and
- future external agencies or partners.

The objective is to create a coherent visual identity that can operate across a serious B2B software platform, corporate communications, websites, applications, documents and future product families.

This document defines **requirements, principles and evaluation criteria**. It does not yet approve a final logo, colour palette, typeface or proprietary visual motif.

## 2. Strategic visual objective

The NuBlox identity should communicate that the business is:

- capable;
- modern;
- practical;
- precise;
- dependable;
- connected;
- technically credible; and
- progressive.

The visual identity should support NuBlox's intended market position as an integrated operating platform for the built environment without relying on conventional construction-industry clichés.

The identity must feel appropriate for both:

- a small specialist contractor adopting business software for the first time; and
- a larger organisation evaluating NuBlox as a long-term operational platform.

## 3. Desired visual perception

When a customer first encounters NuBlox, the desired reaction is approximately:

> **This looks like a serious, modern business platform that understands complex real-world operations.**

The identity should not create the impression of:

- a consumer app;
- a casual startup experiment;
- a generic construction directory;
- a trade-only mobile application;
- an old-style ERP product;
- an architectural practice;
- a building contractor; or
- a technology brand whose visual identity depends on fashionable effects rather than substance.

## 4. Core design principles

### 4.1 Clarity

The identity should remain understandable and recognisable at a glance.

Avoid visual devices that require explanation before they make sense.

### 4.2 Precision

Geometry, spacing, typography and alignment should feel controlled and intentional.

The visual system should reinforce trust in NuBlox as a platform handling business-critical information.

### 4.3 Modularity

The identity should be capable of supporting a portfolio of products and capabilities without creating separate visual brands.

### 4.4 Connection

Where appropriate, visual structure may express connection, joining, systems, relationships, workflow or interoperable components.

This concept should remain subtle rather than literal.

### 4.5 Digital-first behaviour

The identity must work exceptionally well in software interfaces, web applications, browser tabs, mobile screens, application launchers and responsive layouts.

Print use remains important, but digital behaviour takes priority.

### 4.6 Longevity

The identity should avoid dependence on short-lived visual trends.

NuBlox should still look credible after multiple product and technology cycles.

### 4.7 Accessibility

Colour, typography, contrast, interaction states and information hierarchy must be designed for accessibility from the outset rather than corrected later.

## 5. Master-brand visual architecture

NuBlox uses a branded-house architecture.

The visual identity must therefore reinforce **NuBlox** as the dominant brand across the portfolio.

Illustrative hierarchy:

```text
NuBlox
  |
  +-- Projects
  +-- Commercial
  +-- Estimating
  +-- Procurement
  +-- Workforce
  +-- Documents
  +-- Compliance
  +-- Assets
  +-- Finance
  +-- Intelligence
  +-- Integrations
```

Individual products should not receive unrelated logos or visual identities by default.

Product distinction should primarily be achieved through:

- naming;
- information architecture;
- iconography;
- interface context;
- controlled accent treatment where justified; and
- product-specific imagery or diagrams where appropriate.

The master NuBlox identity should remain clearly recognisable throughout.

## 6. Logo system requirements

The NuBlox logo system should eventually contain at least the following approved assets.

### 6.1 Primary wordmark

A clear **NuBlox** wordmark for standard brand use.

The wordmark should:

- remain legible at small sizes;
- work in light and dark environments;
- work in monochrome;
- reproduce cleanly in print and digital formats;
- avoid unnecessary detail;
- avoid appearing childish or gaming-oriented; and
- preserve the canonical spelling **NuBlox**.

### 6.2 Brand symbol

A compact brand symbol should be explored for use where the full wordmark is impractical.

Potential applications include:

- application icon;
- favicon;
- mobile launcher;
- browser/PWA icon;
- avatar;
- loading state;
- watermark;
- product navigation; and
- social profile image.

The symbol should be recognisable independently once brand familiarity is established.

### 6.3 Combined lockup

A standard symbol-and-wordmark lockup should be available if the final design uses a separate symbol.

### 6.4 Corporate descriptor lockup

A restrained secondary treatment may be required for formal contexts using:

> **NuBlox Digital Applications**

The words “Digital Applications” must not compete visually with the NuBlox master brand.

### 6.5 Product lockups

If product lockups are required, they should follow a consistent endorsed structure such as:

```text
NuBlox | Projects
NuBlox | Commercial
NuBlox | Workforce
```

or another approved typographic arrangement.

Separate bespoke logos for each product are not recommended.

## 7. Logo concept territories

The first concept round should explore multiple strategic territories rather than minor variations of one symbol.

### Territory A — Modular system

Concepts based on components, blocks, units or structured modules that combine into a coherent whole.

Relevant ideas:

- modularity;
- extensibility;
- common foundation;
- connected applications;
- building systems; and
- structured data.

Risk to avoid: looking like toy blocks, masonry products or a generic cube logo.

### Territory B — Connected structure

Concepts based on relationships, links, interfaces or connected nodes.

Relevant ideas:

- connected workflows;
- shared information;
- organisations working together;
- interoperability; and
- integrated operations.

Risk to avoid: generic network/AI symbolism.

### Territory C — Constructed monogram

A distinctive symbol derived from **N**, **B**, or **NB**, constructed using a controlled geometric system.

Relevant ideas:

- ownership of a recognisable brand asset;
- digital scalability;
- compact application icon; and
- strong brand recall.

Risk to avoid: forcing too many hidden meanings into one monogram.

### Territory D — Wordmark-led identity

A strong custom NuBlox wordmark with little or no separate symbolism.

Relevant ideas:

- confidence;
- simplicity;
- recognisability;
- typographic ownership; and
- long-term flexibility.

Risk to avoid: becoming too generic if the wordmark is insufficiently distinctive.

## 8. Logo concepts to avoid

Do not default to:

- hard hats;
- cranes;
- excavators;
- houses;
- roof outlines;
- skyscraper silhouettes;
- brick walls;
- construction warning stripes;
- spanners or tools;
- literal blueprints;
- generic cloud symbols;
- generic circuit-board imagery;
- unexplained AI sparkles;
- generic cubes solely because the name contains “Blox”; or
- visual references that make NuBlox appear to be a construction contractor rather than a technology business.

A block or modular idea is permissible only where it expresses the wider platform concept in a distinctive and sophisticated way.

## 9. Wordmark direction

The wordmark should feel contemporary, structured and confident.

Areas to explore include:

- custom letterforms;
- subtle geometric construction;
- controlled relationships between `N`, `u`, `B`, `l`, `o`, `x`;
- intentional treatment of the capital `N` and `B`;
- distinctive spacing; and
- integration between symbol and typography.

The wordmark should not depend on unusual spelling, excessive character modification or poor legibility to become distinctive.

## 10. Typography requirements

Typography is especially important because NuBlox will be a data-dense business application.

The typographic system should support:

- application navigation;
- data tables;
- forms;
- dashboards;
- reports;
- marketing pages;
- long-form documentation;
- numerical and financial information;
- technical identifiers;
- mobile interfaces; and
- accessibility requirements.

### 10.1 Primary typeface characteristics

The primary UI/body typeface should be:

- highly legible;
- contemporary;
- neutral enough for sustained application use;
- distinctive enough to support brand character;
- strong at small sizes;
- suitable for dense data interfaces;
- available in sufficient weights;
- suitable for tabular or financial content; and
- legally/licensably practical for web and application distribution.

### 10.2 Display typography

A separate display face is optional.

If used, it should add character to marketing and brand communications without creating visual conflict with the product UI.

### 10.3 Monospace typography

A monospace typeface may be required for:

- identifiers;
- code snippets;
- API documentation;
- audit records; and
- structured technical values.

This should be considered part of the product design system rather than the core marketing identity.

## 11. Colour strategy

Final brand colours are not approved by this document.

The chosen colour system should satisfy the following requirements.

### 11.1 Primary brand colour

NuBlox should have a recognisable primary colour or tightly controlled primary colour relationship.

It should:

- work in both corporate and product contexts;
- remain credible in enterprise software;
- provide sufficient differentiation from common construction-software conventions;
- support accessible contrast combinations; and
- reproduce reliably across screens and print.

### 11.2 Neutral system

A strong neutral scale is required for the application UI.

The neutral palette must support:

- backgrounds;
- surfaces;
- borders;
- disabled states;
- primary and secondary text;
- data tables;
- form controls; and
- light/dark interface modes if adopted.

### 11.3 Semantic colours

Semantic colours must be functionally separate from brand colours.

The design system will require consistent semantic treatment for states such as:

- success;
- warning;
- error;
- information;
- pending;
- approved;
- rejected;
- overdue; and
- inactive.

A brand colour must not become the sole mechanism for communicating workflow state.

### 11.4 Product accents

Product-specific accents may be explored, but they should not fragment the brand.

If used, they must behave as secondary navigation or categorisation aids rather than separate product identities.

## 12. Initial colour territories to explore

The concept phase may explore several territories before final selection.

### Territory 1 — Deep technical blue / blue-violet

Potential perception:

- dependable;
- technical;
- precise;
- enterprise-capable.

Risk: crowded software category and potential genericness.

### Territory 2 — Distinctive violet / indigo system

Potential perception:

- modern;
- digital;
- differentiated;
- intelligent.

Risk: overuse in contemporary technology brands if not handled distinctively.

### Territory 3 — Teal / blue-green system

Potential perception:

- connected;
- modern;
- operational;
- calm.

Risk: may drift toward fintech, healthcare or sustainability conventions.

### Territory 4 — High-contrast neutral-led identity with a proprietary accent

Potential perception:

- premium;
- serious;
- precise;
- design-led.

Risk: insufficient recognition if the accent colour is weak or inconsistently used.

Construction orange/yellow should not be selected merely because the initial market is construction. Such colours may still be used semantically where appropriate.

## 13. Accessibility requirements

The visual identity and application design system must be capable of meeting WCAG-aligned accessibility requirements.

Design decisions should account for:

- text/background contrast;
- non-text contrast;
- focus visibility;
- colour-blind users;
- reduced motion preferences;
- scalable typography;
- keyboard navigation;
- touch target clarity; and
- information states that do not depend on colour alone.

Brand aesthetics must never override product accessibility.

## 14. Iconography

NuBlox requires a coherent icon system for both product navigation and functional actions.

Iconography should be:

- simple;
- geometric;
- legible at small sizes;
- consistent in stroke and optical weight;
- recognisable without decorative detail; and
- suitable for dense application environments.

Product icons may help distinguish areas such as Projects, Commercial, Procurement and Workforce, but should remain part of one visual family.

Avoid bespoke pictograms where established interface symbols are clearer.

## 15. Illustration and diagram language

Illustration should be used purposefully rather than decoratively.

Useful applications include:

- explaining workflows;
- showing platform relationships;
- visualising lifecycle stages;
- communicating data movement;
- onboarding;
- empty states; and
- product education.

A system of geometric diagrams and structured visualisations is likely to align better with NuBlox than character-based cartoon illustration.

## 16. Photography and imagery

Where photography is used, imagery should represent the breadth of the built environment rather than only active construction sites.

Relevant subjects may include:

- design and planning;
- professional collaboration;
- site activity;
- trades and specialist work;
- surveying;
- engineering;
- manufacturing and supply;
- facilities and maintenance;
- asset operation;
- buildings and infrastructure; and
- real business environments.

Photography should avoid staged clichés where possible.

The human and operational aspects of the sector should be represented alongside built assets.

## 17. Data visualisation

Because NuBlox is an operating platform, data visualisation will become a significant part of the visual language.

The system should support:

- financial charts;
- project performance;
- procurement status;
- workforce metrics;
- programme/schedule information;
- compliance information;
- asset performance; and
- operational dashboards.

Charts should prioritise comprehension over decoration.

Brand colours should not be applied indiscriminately to data series.

A dedicated accessible data-visualisation palette should eventually be defined.

## 18. Product UI relationship

The brand identity and product UI should feel related, but the application must remain functional under sustained daily use.

The product UI should inherit:

- typography;
- primary brand colour;
- neutral system;
- iconography;
- spacing philosophy;
- border/radius philosophy;
- motion principles; and
- tone of visual hierarchy.

It should not attempt to turn every screen into a marketing composition.

### Product design character

The UI should feel:

- structured;
- efficient;
- calm;
- information-rich without being visually noisy;
- consistent;
- predictable; and
- adaptable to different professions and workflows.

## 19. Shape language

A coherent shape language should eventually define:

- corner radii;
- card treatments;
- buttons;
- inputs;
- panels;
- badges;
- chips;
- navigation; and
- diagrams.

The system should avoid both extremes:

- excessively rounded consumer-app styling; and
- severe, outdated enterprise styling.

The desired balance is modern, precise and practical.

## 20. Spacing and grid philosophy

The application should use a systematic spacing scale and responsive grid.

The brand identity should support layouts that feel organised and engineered rather than decorative.

Design tokens should eventually define:

- spacing;
- sizing;
- radii;
- typography;
- colour;
- elevation;
- breakpoints; and
- motion.

These tokens should be implementable directly in the Svelte 5/SvelteKit product.

## 21. Motion principles

Motion should communicate state and continuity rather than act as decoration.

Appropriate use includes:

- navigation transitions;
- loading and progress;
- expanding/collapsing information;
- confirmation;
- workflow movement; and
- relationship between interface states.

Motion should be:

- fast;
- restrained;
- predictable; and
- compatible with reduced-motion accessibility preferences.

## 22. Dark mode

The identity should not depend on a light-only environment.

Logo, colour, typography and component decisions should be tested for potential dark-mode use even if dark mode is not included in the first product release.

At minimum, the brand system must include approved light-on-dark and dark-on-light logo treatments.

## 23. Favicon and application icon

The NuBlox symbol must work at very small sizes.

Required target environments should include:

- 16px browser favicon;
- 32px browser favicon;
- browser/PWA application icon;
- mobile/desktop launcher icon;
- social avatar; and
- repository/profile avatar where appropriate.

A symbol that only works in a large presentation should not be approved.

## 24. Document and business-material application

The identity must support professional business documents including:

- proposals;
- quotations;
- contracts;
- reports;
- invoices;
- technical specifications;
- implementation documents;
- presentation decks;
- training material; and
- policies.

Templates should feel recognisably NuBlox without wasting document space on decorative brand elements.

## 25. Website application

The website should communicate platform breadth while remaining easy to navigate.

The visual system should support:

- master-brand pages;
- product pages;
- profession/audience solution pages;
- integrations;
- resources;
- pricing;
- support; and
- developer/API content.

The website should not visually resemble a building contractor's marketing site.

## 26. Social and campaign application

Campaign assets should derive from the core identity rather than invent a different style for each campaign.

The system should support:

- social profile graphics;
- product announcements;
- feature launches;
- thought-leadership content;
- case studies;
- events; and
- recruitment.

## 27. GitHub and developer identity

NuBlox has a technical audience as well as commercial users.

Repository and developer documentation should use a restrained implementation of the identity that prioritises readability.

Potential assets include:

- repository avatar;
- README header treatment;
- documentation diagrams;
- architecture diagrams;
- API documentation styling; and
- release graphics.

## 29. Technical asset requirements

The final identity package should provide, at minimum:

### Logo assets

- SVG master files;
- PNG exports at required sizes;
- monochrome variants;
- light-background variants;
- dark-background variants;
- symbol-only variants;
- wordmark variants; and
- lockups where approved.

### Digital application assets

- favicon set;
- PWA/application icons;
- social avatar;
- Open Graph/social sharing image templates; and
- email/document logo assets.

### Design source

Editable source design files should be retained in the agreed design platform and linked from the repository documentation rather than committed as uncontrolled duplicates where impractical.

## 30. Evaluation criteria for logo concepts

Every serious logo concept should be evaluated against the same criteria.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Distinctiveness | Is it recognisably NuBlox rather than generic software branding? |
| Relevance | Does it support the brand strategy without relying on literal construction imagery? |
| Simplicity | Can it be recognised quickly and reproduced reliably? |
| Scalability | Does it work from favicon size to signage/presentation scale? |
| Digital suitability | Does it work well inside applications and browser/mobile environments? |
| Typography | Does the wordmark remain highly legible? |
| Portfolio fit | Can it support the full NuBlox product family? |
| Accessibility | Can appropriate contrast and alternate treatments be produced? |
| Longevity | Is it likely to remain credible over time? |
| Ownability | Does it offer enough distinctive character to justify legal/trademark investigation? |

No concept should be selected solely because it looks attractive in a presentation mock-up.

## 31. Concept presentation requirements

Each concept shortlisted for review should be shown in practical contexts including:

- wordmark on light background;
- wordmark on dark background;
- symbol at favicon size;
- application sidebar/header;
- login screen;
- website header;
- proposal/report cover;
- mobile/PWA icon;
- GitHub avatar; and
- at least one product endorsement such as **NuBlox Projects**.

This prevents approval of concepts that fail outside idealised brand boards.

## 32. Visual identity guardrails

NuBlox should not:

- create a different identity for every product;
- use colour alone to distinguish critical application states;
- sacrifice interface clarity for brand decoration;
- rely on construction clichés;
- imitate a competitor's visual language;
- use unlicensed fonts or imagery;
- make the logo overly detailed;
- use visual effects that do not reproduce reliably;
- allow inconsistent unofficial logo modifications; or
- introduce arbitrary design tokens outside the governed design system.

## 33. Legal and clearance requirements

Before final public adoption, the selected identity should undergo appropriate checks including:

- trademark review for the NuBlox name and intended mark;
- company-name considerations where relevant;
- domain and social-handle review;
- similarity review against relevant software and built-environment brands;
- font licensing;
- icon licensing; and
- stock/photography licensing where applicable.

Design approval and legal clearance are separate decisions.

## 34. Proposed design workflow

The recommended sequence is:

1. confirm this visual identity brief;
2. develop multiple genuinely different logo territories;
3. shortlist the strongest concepts;
4. test concepts in real product and corporate contexts;
5. conduct preliminary distinctiveness/conflict review;
6. select a preferred direction;
7. refine wordmark, symbol and geometry;
8. develop colour palette;
9. select typography;
10. define iconography and supporting visual language;
11. perform accessibility checks;
12. build application and web design tokens;
13. complete legal/trademark clearance as appropriate;
14. produce final master assets; and
15. document the final brand guidelines.

## 35. Deliverables following this brief

The next visual-brand deliverables should be developed in this order:

### 05 — Logo concept directions

Several strategically distinct identity concepts with rationale and practical mock-up tests.

### 06 — Colour and typography system

Final or shortlisted palette, typography, accessibility and usage rules.

### 07 — Product design foundations

Design tokens, component visual principles and the relationship between brand and application UI.

### 08 — Brand guidelines

Approved identity usage, logo rules, colour, typography, imagery, iconography, tone and applications.

## 37. Approval state

This document approves the **visual identity design brief**, not a final visual identity.

No final logo, colour, typeface or iconography selection should be treated as approved until it has been reviewed against the requirements in this document.
