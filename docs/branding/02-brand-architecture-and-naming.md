# NuBlox Brand Architecture and Naming

**Document status:** Draft v1.0  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Scope:** Corporate, master-brand, platform, product and capability naming architecture  
**Parent documents:** `docs/branding/00-business-entity.md`, `docs/branding/01-brand-strategy.md`

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document defines how the NuBlox name should be structured and applied across the business, software platform, products, modules, services and future offerings.

The objective is to prevent naming fragmentation as the NuBlox ecosystem grows.

The naming system must:

- preserve a strong and recognisable master brand;
- make products and capabilities easy to understand;
- support multiple built-environment professions and organisation types;
- allow future expansion into adjacent sectors or product families;
- avoid unnecessary sub-brands;
- work consistently in product UI, sales, documentation, support and commercial packaging; and
- remain suitable for future legal, trademark and domain-name clearance.

This document establishes a strategic naming architecture. It does **not** assert trademark availability, company-name availability or domain ownership. Those checks must be performed before names are treated as legally cleared.

## 2. Recommended brand model

NuBlox should use a **branded-house architecture**.

Under this model, **NuBlox** is the dominant commercial brand and most products, applications and services inherit the NuBlox name rather than operating as independent brands.

### Recommended hierarchy

```text
NuBlox Digital Applications
Corporate/legal entity layer — working name
        |
        v
NuBlox
Master commercial brand
        |
        v
NuBlox platform ecosystem
        |
        +-- NuBlox Projects
        +-- NuBlox Commercial
        +-- NuBlox Estimating
        +-- NuBlox Procurement
        +-- NuBlox Workforce
        +-- NuBlox Finance
        +-- NuBlox Assets
        +-- NuBlox CRM
        +-- NuBlox Documents
        +-- NuBlox Compliance
        +-- NuBlox Intelligence
        +-- NuBlox Integrations
        +-- NuBlox Services
```

The names shown above are architectural working names and may be refined as product scope and commercial packaging are formalised.

## 3. Why a branded house is preferred

A branded-house model is preferred because NuBlox is intended to provide connected capabilities on a shared operating foundation.

Creating separate stand-alone brands for every application would work against that proposition by making the ecosystem appear fragmented.

The branded-house model provides:

1. **brand concentration** — marketing investment strengthens one recognisable name;
2. **cross-product trust** — confidence in NuBlox can transfer to new capabilities;
3. **portfolio clarity** — customers can see that products belong to one platform;
4. **simpler navigation** — product names describe functions rather than requiring customers to learn invented names;
5. **commercial flexibility** — capabilities can be packaged separately without becoming disconnected brands; and
6. **future scalability** — new products can be introduced through a repeatable naming rule.

## 4. Brand layers

NuBlox should distinguish six naming layers.

### Layer 1 — Legal/corporate entity

**Working name:** NuBlox Digital Applications

This is the corporate or legal-business layer.

It may appear on:

- legal documents;
- contracts;
- invoices;
- regulatory filings;
- employment documentation;
- corporate policies;
- supplier records; and
- formal company information.

The final legal name remains subject to incorporation, company-name and trademark considerations.

### Layer 2 — Master brand

**Name:** NuBlox

This is the principal customer-facing brand.

NuBlox should appear prominently across:

- the website;
- software;
- sales and marketing;
- documentation;
- support;
- customer communications;
- partnerships; and
- product launches.

The master brand should carry the reputation, visual identity, personality and strategic positioning defined in `01-brand-strategy.md`.

### Layer 3 — Platform descriptor

**Working descriptor:** NuBlox Platform

“NuBlox Platform” should primarily describe the shared software ecosystem rather than become a visually separate sub-brand.

It refers to the common foundation supporting:

- identity and access;
- organisations and parties;
- shared master data;
- workflow;
- permissions;
- collaboration;
- documents and records;
- reporting;
- automation;
- integrations;
- APIs; and
- domain applications.

In many customer contexts, **NuBlox** alone should be sufficient. “Platform” should be used where the distinction between the overall ecosystem and a specific application needs to be explicit.

### Layer 4 — Product/application families

Customer-recognisable product families should follow the pattern:

> **NuBlox + functional descriptor**

Examples:

- NuBlox Projects
- NuBlox Commercial
- NuBlox Estimating
- NuBlox Procurement
- NuBlox Workforce
- NuBlox Finance
- NuBlox Assets
- NuBlox CRM
- NuBlox Documents
- NuBlox Compliance

These names should represent coherent areas of customer value rather than every internal software domain.

### Layer 5 — Features and capabilities

Features should normally use plain functional names without repeating “NuBlox” at every level.

Example:

```text
NuBlox Commercial
  ├── Cost Plans
  ├── Budgets
  ├── Commitments
  ├── Variations
  ├── Applications
  ├── Valuations
  ├── Forecasts
  └── Cost Reports
```

This keeps the product understandable and prevents naming clutter.

### Layer 6 — Services, integrations and specialist offerings

Supporting offerings should remain clearly endorsed by the master brand.

Examples:

- NuBlox Services
- NuBlox Implementation
- NuBlox Migration
- NuBlox Integrations
- NuBlox API
- NuBlox Academy — only if a substantial training/learning proposition is later created

New service names should only be introduced when they represent a genuine customer-facing proposition, not an internal team or process.

## 5. Core naming principle

The default rule is:

> **Use NuBlox for the brand, clear language for the function, and invented names only when there is a strong strategic reason.**

The customer should not need a glossary simply to understand what each product does.

## 6. Recommended product naming pattern

### External/customer-facing naming

Use the endorsed pattern:

> **NuBlox [Product]**

For example:

- NuBlox Projects
- NuBlox Procurement
- NuBlox Workforce

This should be used in:

- pricing;
- sales material;
- product pages;
- proposals;
- contracts;
- release announcements; and
- formal documentation.

### In-product navigation

Inside the application, once the user is clearly within the NuBlox environment, navigation may use the shorter descriptors:

- Projects
- Commercial
- Estimating
- Procurement
- Workforce
- Assets

Repeating the master brand in every menu label would add visual noise without improving comprehension.

### Formal first reference

A document or page should generally use the full name on first meaningful reference and may shorten it afterwards where context is unambiguous.

Example:

> NuBlox Procurement provides purchasing and supplier workflows. Procurement shares organisation, project and commercial records with the wider NuBlox platform.

## 7. Proposed initial product/application architecture

The following architecture is recommended as a customer-facing starting point.

| Working product name | Primary scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **NuBlox CRM** | customers, organisations, contacts, opportunities and relationships | Shared commercial/party foundation |
| **NuBlox Estimating** | estimates, take-off inputs, pricing, quotations and tenders | May later include specialist estimating capability packs |
| **NuBlox Projects** | project setup, coordination, delivery and project controls | Broad project workspace |
| **NuBlox Commercial** | budgets, cost control, commitments, valuations, variations and forecasting | Strong built-environment commercial identity |
| **NuBlox Procurement** | suppliers, RFQs, purchasing, orders and procurement workflows | Connected to estimating, projects and commercial |
| **NuBlox Workforce** | people, competency, time, scheduling and workforce administration | Should not imply a full HR/payroll product until delivered |
| **NuBlox Documents** | project/business documents, revisions, records and controlled information | Naming may later be reviewed against information-management scope |
| **NuBlox Compliance** | quality, safety, inspections, RAMS, actions and compliance records | Potential umbrella for several compliance domains |
| **NuBlox Assets** | assets, maintenance, work orders and lifecycle records | Supports operational stage of built assets |
| **NuBlox Finance** | financial/business accounting capabilities where delivered | Must not imply unsupported accounting scope |
| **NuBlox Intelligence** | reporting, analytics, insights and future intelligent assistance | Use only when meaningful capabilities exist |
| **NuBlox Integrations** | external-system connections and integration management | Platform-level capability |

This architecture is intentionally customer-oriented. It does not need to map one-to-one with internal bounded contexts, database packages or code modules.

## 8. Product architecture versus software architecture

Brand architecture and technical architecture must remain related but distinct.

For example, the database may contain separate bounded contexts for:

- parties;
- quotations;
- contracts;
- procurement;
- workforce;
- documents;
- site operations;
- commercial cost control; and
- assets.

Customers do not need to see every internal boundary as a separately branded product.

A customer-facing product such as **NuBlox Projects** may compose functionality from several technical domains.

Conversely, a shared technical service such as permissions may support every product without needing a customer-facing product name.

## 9. Capability packs

NuBlox may use **capability packs** to tailor the platform to professions, organisation types or specialist workflows without creating separate software products for every career.

Recommended pattern:

> **[Profession/discipline] capabilities for NuBlox**

or, where commercially useful:

> **NuBlox for [audience]**

Examples:

- NuBlox for Quantity Surveyors
- NuBlox for Architects
- NuBlox for Electrical Contractors
- NuBlox for Facilities Management

These should initially be treated as **audience propositions/configurations**, not stand-alone brands.

They may combine existing modules, templates, workflows, terminology and permissions appropriate to that audience.

This directly supports the governing product rule:

> **NuBlox models what people and organisations do, not only what their job title is.**

## 10. “NuBlox for…” naming

“NuBlox for [Audience]” is useful for marketing and solution packaging because it allows NuBlox to speak directly to specific professions without fragmenting the platform.

Examples:

- NuBlox for Contractors
- NuBlox for Subcontractors
- NuBlox for Surveyors
- NuBlox for Consultants
- NuBlox for Asset Operators

The expression should mean:

> a configured combination of NuBlox capabilities relevant to that audience.

It should **not** automatically imply a separately developed codebase or independent product.

## 11. Industry and future-sector architecture

The corporate brand should retain the ability to expand beyond construction and the built environment.

If NuBlox later enters substantially different industries, the preferred sequence is:

```text
NuBlox
  |
  +-- Built Environment solutions
  +-- [Future industry] solutions
```

The business should first test whether simple audience or industry descriptors are sufficient before creating formal sector sub-brands.

A new endorsed sector brand should only be considered where:

- the market has materially different buyers and language;
- the product proposition is substantially distinct;
- commercial separation provides a measurable advantage; or
- legal/regulatory factors justify separation.

## 12. Naming syntax

### Master brand spelling

The canonical spelling is:

**NuBlox**

Rules:

- capital **N**;
- capital **B**;
- no space between “Nu” and “Blox”;
- no hyphen;
- do not use “Nublox”, “Nu Blox” or “NUBLOX” as normal prose styling unless an approved visual identity treatment specifically uses capitals.

### Product names

Use title case:

- NuBlox Projects
- NuBlox Commercial
- NuBlox Workforce

### Feature names

Use normal title case where the feature acts as a product-navigation label:

- Purchase Orders
- Cost Reports
- Project Documents
- Site Inspections

Avoid excessive proprietary naming for ordinary functions.

## 13. Naming criteria

Every proposed customer-facing name should be assessed against the following criteria.

### Clarity

Can the intended customer understand what the offering is likely to do?

### Fit

Does the name belong naturally within the NuBlox portfolio?

### Distinctiveness

Is it sufficiently distinctive in the relevant market and context?

### Extensibility

Can the name accommodate reasonable future scope without becoming misleading?

### International usability

Is it pronounceable, understandable and culturally appropriate in likely future markets?

### Digital usability

Does it work in URLs, navigation, search, documentation and product interfaces?

### Legal defensibility

Can it potentially be protected or safely used after appropriate legal and trademark checks?

### Longevity

Is it likely to remain credible beyond a short-lived technology or marketing trend?

## 14. Names to avoid

NuBlox should generally avoid:

- unexplained invented sub-brand names;
- acronyms that customers must decode;
- overly narrow names that block future product expansion;
- technology-specific names likely to age quickly;
- names implying capabilities not actually provided;
- visually clever spellings that reduce readability;
- construction clichés used solely for effect;
- names that suggest each module is a separate disconnected product; and
- names chosen before trademark and domain checks where the name is intended for public commercial use.

## 15. The role of “Digital Applications”

“Digital Applications” should currently function as a **corporate descriptor**, not the everyday customer brand.

Preferred:

```text
Corporate/legal context: NuBlox Digital Applications
Commercial/customer context: NuBlox
```

Do not routinely name products using the full corporate form.

Avoid:

- NuBlox Digital Applications Projects
- NuBlox Digital Applications Procurement
- NuBlox Digital Applications Workforce

These are unnecessarily long and weaken the master brand.

## 16. The role of “Built Environment Business Operating System”

“Built Environment Business Operating System” is a useful category/positioning description but should not be treated as the formal product name at this stage.

It can explain the strategic scope of NuBlox in investor, developer or product-definition contexts.

Customer-facing language should be tested for clarity because “operating system” may be interpreted technically rather than commercially by some audiences.

Preferred distinction:

- **Brand:** NuBlox
- **Category/positioning language:** operating platform for the built environment
- **Product/application names:** NuBlox Projects, NuBlox Commercial, etc.

## 17. Naming future automation and intelligent capabilities

Artificial-intelligence or automation features should not automatically receive a personified assistant name.

Default naming should remain functional, for example:

- NuBlox Intelligence
- Insights
- Automation
- Assisted Review
- Suggested Actions

A distinct assistant name should only be created if the capability develops into a sufficiently important, coherent and persistent user experience to justify its own identity.

This avoids unnecessary novelty branding and keeps customer expectations grounded in actual capability.

## 18. Integration naming

External-system integrations should use the external service name descriptively with appropriate trademark attribution where required.

Pattern:

> **[External service] integration for NuBlox**

or in product UI:

> **Integrations → [External service]**

Integration partners should not appear to be owned by, merged with or formally endorsed by NuBlox unless such a relationship actually exists.

## 19. Commercial package naming

Subscription tiers and commercial bundles should be separated from product names.

For example, a customer may subscribe to several NuBlox products through a commercial package.

The architecture should distinguish:

```text
Brand
  NuBlox

Products
  Projects / Commercial / Procurement / Workforce

Packages
  Commercial subscription bundles to be defined later
```

Avoid using product names and pricing-tier names interchangeably.

Final package naming belongs in the pricing and packaging strategy.

## 20. Edition naming

If editions become necessary, prefer clear descriptors over status-based labels.

Possible structure:

- NuBlox [Product] — Standard
- NuBlox [Product] — Professional
- NuBlox [Product] — Enterprise

However, edition naming should not be finalised until packaging, entitlements and target segments are known.

Avoid inventing artificial differences solely to create more tiers.

## 21. URL and route naming implications

The naming model should influence information architecture while remaining implementation-friendly.

Illustrative public routes:

```text
/products/projects
/products/commercial
/products/procurement
/products/workforce
/solutions/contractors
/solutions/quantity-surveyors
/integrations
```

Illustrative in-application routes may use functional domain names without repeating the brand:

```text
/projects
/commercial
/procurement
/workforce
/assets
```

Technical route design remains an application architecture decision, but customer-facing naming should remain consistent across routes, UI and documentation.

## 22. Domain-name strategy principles

The preferred digital strategy is to concentrate authority under a primary NuBlox domain rather than acquire a separate primary domain for every product.

Recommended pattern:

```text
Primary corporate/product domain
  ├── /products/...
  ├── /solutions/...
  ├── /resources/...
  ├── /docs/...
  └── /support/...
```

Product-specific domains may be defensively registered where commercially justified but should normally redirect to the main brand ecosystem.

No domain should be treated as available until checked and acquired.

## 23. Trademark strategy principles

Before public commercial launch, NuBlox should assess protection for at least:

- the NuBlox word mark;
- the NuBlox logo/device mark once created;
- the final corporate name where relevant;
- any strategically important product names that are sufficiently distinctive; and
- relevant classes and geographic markets.

Descriptive module names such as “Projects” or “Procurement” are primarily portfolio descriptors and may not themselves provide strong standalone trademark distinctiveness.

The master-brand strategy therefore makes protection and reputation of **NuBlox** especially important.

## 24. Brand endorsement rules

All major products should visibly belong to NuBlox.

### Preferred

**NuBlox Commercial**

**NuBlox Projects**

**NuBlox Workforce**

### Avoid

Creating visually unrelated names such as:

```text
BuildFlow
Quantix
CrewSphere
AssetNova
```

and expecting customers to infer that they are parts of NuBlox.

Independent names should only be introduced where there is a documented strategic case approved as an exception to the branded-house model.

## 25. Acquisition or partner-brand exceptions

If NuBlox later acquires another product or forms a material joint proposition, transition architecture may be required.

Possible temporary approaches include:

- “[Product] by NuBlox”;
- “NuBlox + [Partner]”; or
- retaining an acquired name during a controlled migration period.

These are exceptions, not the default architecture.

## 26. Visual identity implications

The visual system should reinforce the brand hierarchy.

### Master brand

The principal NuBlox wordmark/symbol receives the strongest identity treatment.

### Product families

Products should generally inherit:

- the NuBlox logo;
- common typography;
- common colour foundations;
- common iconography;
- common interface patterns; and
- a consistent naming lock-up where required.

Product differentiation should not depend on creating a different logo for every module.

### Features

Features should be represented primarily through UI labels and icons, not separate logos.

## 27. Naming governance

New names should not be introduced ad hoc by individual development, marketing or sales workstreams.

Any new customer-facing name should document:

1. what is being named;
2. why an existing descriptor is insufficient;
3. intended audience;
4. relationship to NuBlox;
5. proposed canonical spelling;
6. potential conflicts with existing portfolio names;
7. digital/domain considerations;
8. legal/trademark screening status; and
9. the owner responsible for approving the name.

A lightweight naming register should be created as the portfolio becomes active.

## 28. Recommended canonical hierarchy

The current recommended hierarchy is:

```text
NuBlox Digital Applications
  Corporate/legal business — working name

NuBlox
  Master brand

NuBlox Platform
  Descriptive name for the shared operating ecosystem

NuBlox [Product]
  Customer-facing application/product family

[Feature]
  Plain-language product capability

NuBlox for [Audience]
  Solution configuration / market proposition
```

## 29. Working portfolio

The current working portfolio is:

```text
NuBlox
├── CRM
├── Estimating
├── Projects
├── Commercial
├── Procurement
├── Workforce
├── Documents
├── Compliance
├── Assets
├── Finance
├── Intelligence
└── Integrations
```

These names are a strategic framework rather than a commitment that each item launches as a separately priced product.

Product boundaries should ultimately be reconciled with customer research, application architecture, entitlements and pricing strategy.

## 30. Decisions made by this document

Unless later superseded, the following decisions should guide subsequent branding work:

1. **NuBlox is the master commercial brand.**
2. **NuBlox Digital Applications remains the working corporate/legal-business name.**
3. **NuBlox should use a branded-house architecture.**
4. **Products should default to the format “NuBlox + functional descriptor”.**
5. **Features should use clear functional names rather than independent brands.**
6. **“NuBlox for [Audience]” should be used for audience solutions/configurations, not separate codebases by default.**
7. **NuBlox Platform is a descriptive ecosystem term, not a competing master brand.**
8. **Internal technical modules do not require one-to-one customer-facing product brands.**
9. **The visual identity should be shared across the portfolio.**
10. **Public names remain subject to appropriate legal, trademark and domain checks before commercial adoption.**
