# NuBlox Logo Concept Directions

**Document status:** Draft v1.0  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Scope:** First-round logo and identity concept territories for NuBlox  
**Parent document:** `docs/branding/04-visual-identity-brief.md`

## 1. Purpose

This document converts the approved visual-identity brief into a structured first concept round for the NuBlox master brand.

The purpose of the first round is **not** to select a finished logo immediately. It is to test meaningfully different visual territories against the business and brand strategy, identify the strongest strategic direction, and then refine that direction through subsequent design iterations.

All concepts remain exploratory until they have passed visual, usability and legal/trademark review.

## 2. Common requirements for every concept

Every concept must preserve the canonical brand spelling **NuBlox** and must be capable of producing:

- a primary wordmark;
- a compact standalone symbol;
- a symbol-and-wordmark lockup;
- a monochrome version;
- light-background and dark-background versions;
- a favicon/application icon;
- a corporate `NuBlox Digital Applications` lockup;
- consistent product lockups such as `NuBlox Projects` and `NuBlox Commercial`; and
- assets suitable for responsive web, Svelte application UI, documentation and print.

Every concept should communicate, in differing proportions:

- capability;
- precision;
- connection;
- modularity;
- practical technology;
- enterprise credibility; and
- long-term scalability.

## 3. Concept A — Modular Nexus

### Strategic idea

NuBlox is a connected operating foundation made from interoperable capabilities rather than isolated software products.

### Visual territory

A compact geometric symbol constructed from a small number of interlocking or adjoining modules. The modules should form a distinctive whole without becoming a literal stack of toy blocks or a generic isometric cube.

Possible structural ideas include:

- two or three geometric units sharing edges;
- negative space creating an `N` or `B`;
- modules converging around a common centre;
- an open structure implying extensibility; and
- a repeatable geometry that can influence diagrams, UI motifs and motion.

### Desired perception

**Structured, connected, extensible, engineered.**

### Strengths

- directly supports the platform proposition;
- relates subtly to the `Blox` name without becoming literal;
- strong potential for a standalone application icon;
- can extend into a broader design language; and
- works well with the branded-house architecture.

### Risks

- generic cube/block symbolism;
- resemblance to construction-material suppliers;
- excessive 3D treatment;
- visual complexity at favicon scale.

### Design guardrail

The concept should feel like a **system of connected digital capabilities**, not masonry or children's building blocks.

## 4. Concept B — Connected Framework

### Strategic idea

NuBlox connects people, organisations, processes and information through shared workflows and records.

### Visual territory

A two-dimensional geometric framework in which lines, paths or structural elements connect into a controlled symbol.

The connection should be expressed through geometry rather than generic network dots.

Possible structural ideas include:

- joined paths forming a compact mark;
- interlocking frames;
- shared junctions;
- a continuous path creating an abstract `N`;
- two structures becoming one; and
- positive/negative-space relationships representing shared data.

### Desired perception

**Connected, coordinated, precise, interoperable.**

### Strengths

- strongly supports the central brand theme of connection;
- can communicate cross-organisation and cross-functional workflow;
- naturally suited to motion and workflow animation;
- avoids over-reliance on construction imagery.

### Risks

- generic technology/network appearance;
- accidental resemblance to AI or blockchain logos;
- thin-line symbols may fail at small sizes.

### Design guardrail

Use **structural connection**, not generic node-and-network imagery.

## 5. Concept C — Constructed NB Monogram

### Strategic idea

Create a proprietary symbol with stronger long-term brand ownership by constructing a monogram from the initials `N` and `B`.

### Visual territory

A compact geometric monogram in which `N` and `B` are integrated through a shared grid, stroke or negative-space system.

The letterforms should not need to be immediately obvious to work as a symbol, but the construction should remain simple enough for small-size recognition.

Possible structural ideas include:

- a shared vertical stem;
- `N` geometry cut into a `B` structure;
- mirrored or rotational construction;
- a single continuous form;
- negative space revealing one of the letters; and
- modular geometry derived from a common unit.

### Desired perception

**Owned, confident, technical, compact.**

### Strengths

- greater potential for proprietary recognition;
- ideal for favicon and application-icon use;
- not dependent on the construction market;
- can scale with NuBlox into other industries.

### Risks

- forced hidden meanings;
- illegible initials;
- monogram may become visually aggressive or overly corporate;
- similarity to existing technology marks must be carefully checked.

### Design guardrail

Prioritise **clarity and memorable silhouette** over clever letter puzzles.

## 6. Concept D — Precision Wordmark

### Strategic idea

Let the distinctive name **NuBlox** carry the identity through a carefully constructed proprietary wordmark rather than depending on a separate symbolic metaphor.

### Visual territory

A clean, contemporary custom wordmark with restrained modifications to selected characters and spacing.

Potential areas of customisation include:

- a distinctive `N` construction;
- relationship between the `u` and capital `B`;
- controlled geometry within `B`;
- a proprietary `x` termination;
- engineered spacing; and
- an optional compact mark extracted from a distinctive wordmark feature.

### Desired perception

**Confident, simple, mature, product-led.**

### Strengths

- maximum emphasis on the NuBlox name;
- low conceptual clutter;
- strong enterprise and software credibility;
- adaptable across future sectors;
- straightforward product endorsement system.

### Risks

- insufficient differentiation if typography is too generic;
- may provide a weaker favicon/app icon unless a secondary symbol emerges;
- customisation can damage readability if overdone.

### Design guardrail

Distinctiveness should come from **proportion, spacing and controlled letterform design**, not novelty typography.

## 7. First-round visual treatment

The first concept round should primarily be reviewed in monochrome or a restrained neutral presentation.

This prevents colour preference from masking weaknesses in the underlying mark.

After structural comparison, shortlisted concepts should be tested across several colour territories from the visual-identity brief:

1. deep technical blue / blue-violet;
2. distinctive indigo / violet;
3. teal / blue-green; and
4. neutral-led identity with a proprietary accent.

Colour should not be used to rescue an otherwise weak mark.

## 8. Concept presentation requirements

Each concept should be presented consistently with:

1. standalone symbol;
2. `NuBlox` horizontal lockup;
3. monochrome positive version;
4. reversed/dark-background version;
5. small app-icon/favicon simulation;
6. example `NuBlox Projects` lockup; and
7. a simple application-header or website-header context.

Comparing all concepts through the same applications is essential.

## 9. Evaluation criteria

Concepts should be scored from **1 to 5** against the following criteria.

| Criterion | What is being tested |
|---|---|
| Strategic fit | Does it reflect the NuBlox business and brand strategy? |
| Distinctiveness | Is the silhouette and identity memorable? |
| Name fit | Does it feel naturally associated with `NuBlox`? |
| Enterprise credibility | Does it look suitable for serious B2B software? |
| Digital scalability | Does it work from favicon to large display? |
| Wordmark legibility | Is `NuBlox` immediately readable? |
| Monochrome performance | Does it work without colour/effects? |
| Product-system fit | Can it endorse Projects, Commercial, Workforce, etc.? |
| UI compatibility | Does it sit comfortably in a dense application interface? |
| International longevity | Is it likely to remain credible across markets and time? |
| Reproducibility | Can it be implemented reliably in SVG, print and screen? |
| Ownability potential | Does it appear sufficiently distinctive to justify legal clearance work? |

No concept should advance simply because it is visually attractive. It must satisfy the operating requirements of the brand.

## 10. Initial strategic preference

Before visual testing, the strongest strategic territories are expected to be:

1. **Constructed NB Monogram** — strongest potential for proprietary brand recognition;
2. **Modular Nexus** — strongest direct expression of platform architecture;
3. **Precision Wordmark** — strongest simplicity and master-brand emphasis; and
4. **Connected Framework** — strongest expression of connected operations but with the highest risk of generic technology symbolism.

This ranking is provisional and must not substitute for visual evaluation.

## 11. What should not advance

Reject concepts that:

- rely on a generic cube with no proprietary construction;
- resemble a building contractor, architect or materials supplier;
- use literal hard-hat, crane, roof, brick or skyline imagery;
- depend on gradients, shadows or 3D effects to remain recognisable;
- cannot be reproduced clearly in one colour;
- become illegible at favicon size;
- distort the spelling `NuBlox`;
- create separate identities for individual products;
- resemble generic AI/network/blockchain branding; or
- communicate novelty at the expense of trust and longevity.

## 12. Refinement process

The intended logo-development sequence is:

```text
Four genuinely different territories
        ↓
Common-context comparison
        ↓
Score against brand criteria
        ↓
Select one or two directions
        ↓
Geometric and typographic refinement
        ↓
Small-size and accessibility testing
        ↓
Colour-system exploration
        ↓
Product-lockup testing
        ↓
Similarity / trademark clearance
        ↓
Final identity system
```

## 13. Deliverables after direction selection

Once a direction is selected, the next identity work should define:

- final symbol geometry;
- final NuBlox wordmark;
- clear-space rules;
- minimum sizes;
- approved lockups;
- colour palette;
- typography system;
- application icon and favicon;
- product endorsement rules;
- incorrect-use examples;
- SVG asset specifications;
- design-token relationship; and
- final brand-guidelines documentation.

## 14. Current status

The strategic brief and concept territories are now sufficiently defined to begin visual concept generation.

The next decision should be based on **visible concepts**, not additional abstract branding theory.
