# NuBlox Verbal Identity and Messaging

**Document status:** Draft v1.0  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Scope:** Verbal identity, messaging hierarchy, audience messaging, product language and copy standards  
**Parent documents:** `docs/branding/00-business-entity.md`, `docs/branding/01-brand-strategy.md`, `docs/branding/02-brand-architecture-and-naming.md`

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document defines how NuBlox should communicate in words.

It translates the business strategy and brand architecture into a repeatable verbal system for:

- the corporate website;
- product pages;
- sales and proposal material;
- onboarding;
- product UX and UI;
- documentation;
- support content;
- partner communications;
- social and campaign content;
- investor or stakeholder material; and
- future product launches.

The goal is consistency without making all communications sound identical.

NuBlox should sound like a competent operating partner: clear, practical, precise and commercially aware.

## 2. Verbal identity principles

NuBlox communication should follow six principles.

### 2.1 Clear before clever

The audience should understand the message on first reading.

Prefer:

> Connect estimating, procurement, projects and commercial control through shared business records.

Avoid:

> Reimagine tomorrow through a next-generation digital ecosystem.

### 2.2 Operational before abstract

Explain what improves in the customer's work.

Prefer language about:

- reducing duplicate entry;
- linking related records;
- controlling project cost;
- managing suppliers;
- recording site activity;
- coordinating people;
- tracing decisions; and
- improving visibility.

Avoid relying on abstract claims such as "transformation", "innovation" or "synergy" unless the statement explains what they mean.

### 2.3 Specific before exaggerated

NuBlox should make claims that can be understood and, eventually, evidenced.

Prefer:

> Keep project, supplier and commercial information connected.

Avoid:

> Revolutionise your entire business instantly.

### 2.4 Confident without overclaiming

Use direct language but distinguish between:

- capabilities that exist;
- capabilities being developed;
- intended platform direction; and
- future possibilities.

Marketing must not present roadmap concepts as currently available functionality.

### 2.5 Industry-aware without excluding people

NuBlox should use terminology familiar to the built environment where it improves clarity, while avoiding unnecessary jargon.

The language should work for:

- contractors;
- subcontractors;
- consultants;
- designers;
- surveyors;
- trades;
- suppliers;
- clients;
- asset owners; and
- operators.

### 2.6 Human without becoming casual

NuBlox should sound professional and approachable.

Use normal language and active sentences.

Avoid sounding bureaucratic, theatrical, overly corporate or artificially conversational.

## 3. Core verbal character

The NuBlox voice should be:

| Characteristic | Meaning in language | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Capable** | Communicate control, breadth and operational understanding | boasting or grandiosity |
| **Practical** | Explain real tasks, workflows and outcomes | abstract technology language |
| **Precise** | Use exact terms and clear distinctions | vague claims and filler |
| **Modern** | Use concise contemporary business language | trend jargon and forced informality |
| **Dependable** | Communicate traceability, structure and control | unsupported guarantees |
| **Progressive** | Show better ways of working | novelty for novelty's sake |
| **Connected** | Explain relationships between people, records and workflows | generic collaboration claims |

## 4. Master message hierarchy

NuBlox communications should work from a consistent hierarchy.

### Level 1 — Category idea

> **Connected business operations for the built environment.**

This is the clearest current statement of the territory NuBlox intends to occupy.

### Level 2 — Strategic proposition

> **NuBlox connects the people, projects, commercial processes and operational information that built-environment businesses depend on.**

### Level 3 — Customer value

> **Reduce fragmented processes, duplicate administration and disconnected information by managing related work through one shared operating platform.**

### Level 4 — Product proof

Product-level communications should then explain the specific capabilities that support the proposition, for example:

- shared customer, supplier and project records;
- estimating and quotation workflows;
- procurement and purchasing;
- project delivery;
- commercial cost control;
- workforce and time management;
- document and information control;
- quality, safety and compliance records;
- asset and maintenance workflows; and
- reporting, automation and integrations as delivered.

## 5. Working headline territories

The following are messaging territories, not legally or commercially cleared taglines.

### Primary strategic line

> **Connected business operations for the built environment.**

Strengths:

- clear;
- broad;
- descriptive;
- suitable across multiple audiences; and
- aligned with the platform proposition.

### Action-oriented line

> **Connect the work. Control the business.**

Strengths:

- concise;
- commercially focused;
- useful in campaigns or presentations; and
- reinforces connection and control.

### Platform-positioning line

> **The operating platform for the built environment.**

Strengths:

- communicates breadth;
- differentiates from a narrow point solution; and
- supports the long-term platform position.

This wording should be market- and trademark-checked before adoption as a master tagline.

### Supporting territory

> **One connected platform for the work behind the build.**

This is more expressive but less precise than the primary strategic line.

## 6. Recommended master descriptor

Until formal tagline selection and clearance are completed, use the following descriptive line where needed:

> **NuBlox is an integrated business operating platform for construction and the built environment.**

This is a category description rather than a slogan.

### 7.1 One-line description

> **NuBlox develops connected business applications for construction and the built environment.**

### 7.2 Short company description

> **NuBlox is a digital technology business developing an integrated operating platform for construction and the built environment, connecting project, commercial, workforce and business processes through shared data and workflows.**

### 7.3 Standard company description

> **NuBlox develops connected business applications for construction and the built environment. Its platform is designed to bring project delivery, commercial management, procurement, workforce activity, information and wider business operations into one structured environment, reducing duplicated administration and improving operational visibility.**

### 7.4 Extended company description

> **NuBlox is a digital technology business developing an integrated operating platform for organisations and professionals across construction and the built environment. The platform is designed around the way real businesses and project teams work, connecting customers, suppliers, projects, commercial processes, workforce activity, documents, compliance records and assets through shared structured data and controlled workflows. Rather than adding another isolated application, NuBlox aims to reduce fragmentation between the systems, spreadsheets and manual processes that organisations already depend on.**

## 8. Elevator pitch

> **Built-environment businesses often run through a mixture of specialist software, spreadsheets, email and manual processes. NuBlox brings related business and project workflows onto one connected platform, so information can move between estimating, procurement, projects, commercial control, workforce activity and other operational functions without being repeatedly recreated or reconciled.**

## 9. Core customer problem language

NuBlox should consistently describe the central problem as **operational fragmentation**.

Useful language includes:

- disconnected systems;
- duplicate administration;
- repeated data entry;
- information silos;
- spreadsheet dependency;
- fragmented project histories;
- weak workflow visibility;
- inconsistent records;
- manual reconciliation;
- disconnected commercial and operational information; and
- business-critical context trapped in email or individual files.

Avoid implying that spreadsheets, email or specialist applications are inherently bad. The problem is inappropriate dependence and lack of connection between systems.

## 10. Core outcome language

NuBlox should focus on the following customer outcomes.

### Connect

- connect related business functions;
- share structured records;
- keep project and business information in context;
- reduce unnecessary re-entry; and
- link workflows across departments and organisations.

### Control

- understand workflow status;
- maintain permissions and accountability;
- track commercial commitments;
- keep traceable operational records; and
- improve governance.

### Simplify

- reduce system switching;
- remove duplicate administrative steps;
- standardise repeatable workflows; and
- make information easier to find and reuse.

### See

- improve operational visibility;
- report from connected records;
- understand project and business position; and
- surface exceptions and required action.

### Grow

- make processes repeatable;
- onboard people more consistently;
- reduce dependency on informal knowledge; and
- extend capability without creating more disconnected systems.

## 11. Messaging pillars

### Pillar 1 — Connected operations

**Core message:** Related work should share context rather than live in separate systems.

Supporting statements:

- Connect business and project workflows through shared records.
- Keep customer, supplier, project and commercial information related.
- Reduce hand-offs that depend on copying data between applications.

### Pillar 2 — Built around real work

**Core message:** NuBlox is structured around actual capabilities and workflows across the built environment.

Supporting statements:

- Configure capability around what people do.
- Support different professions without forcing every organisation into one rigid process.
- Use built-environment language where the work requires it.

### Pillar 3 — Control without needless complexity

**Core message:** Structure, permissions and traceability should improve control without burying users in administration.

Supporting statements:

- Give each workflow clear state and ownership.
- Keep critical records traceable.
- Make governance visible rather than hidden in process manuals.

### Pillar 4 — One foundation, multiple capabilities

**Core message:** Specialist functions become more useful when they operate on a common foundation.

Supporting statements:

- Add capability without creating another information silo.
- Reuse shared organisation, project and party records.
- Keep integrations part of the operating model where specialist systems remain appropriate.

### Pillar 5 — Structured information for better decisions

**Core message:** Operational records should become usable business information.

Supporting statements:

- Report from connected structured records.
- Reduce the effort spent reconciling competing versions of information.
- Improve visibility across operational and commercial activity.

## 12. Audience messaging framework

The master brand remains constant, but emphasis should change by audience.

### 12.1 Business owners and directors

**Lead concern:** control, efficiency, risk and growth.

**Message:**

> Bring the systems and processes behind your projects and business into a more connected operating environment, giving you clearer information and less duplicated administration.

Emphasise:

- management visibility;
- scalable processes;
- reduced fragmentation;
- cost and commercial control;
- governance; and
- fewer disconnected systems.

### 12.2 Contractors and main contractors

**Lead concern:** connecting pre-construction, delivery and commercial control.

**Message:**

> Connect estimating, procurement, projects, site records, workforce activity and commercial control without repeatedly rebuilding the same project information in different systems.

Emphasise:

- tender-to-project continuity;
- procurement;
- cost control;
- site records;
- subcontract and supplier information;
- workforce; and
- project visibility.

### 12.3 Specialist contractors and subcontractors

**Lead concern:** operational control without heavyweight enterprise complexity.

**Message:**

> Run enquiries, quotations, jobs, purchasing, labour, site records and commercial activity through one connected platform designed around the way specialist contractors work.

Emphasise:

- quotations;
- job costing;
- purchasing;
- labour and time;
- variations;
- compliance;
- cash and commercial visibility; and
- practical usability.

### 12.4 Quantity surveyors and commercial teams

**Lead concern:** commercial traceability and reliable project information.

**Message:**

> Keep estimates, budgets, commitments, variations, valuations and forecasts connected to the wider project record instead of managing commercial control as a separate information island.

Emphasise:

- cost plans;
- budgets;
- commitments;
- variations;
- valuations;
- forecasting;
- audit trails; and
- reporting.

### 12.5 Architects, engineers and consultants

**Lead concern:** project information, resources, clients and fees.

**Message:**

Emphasise:

- CRM;
- proposals;
- project coordination;
- information management;
- resources;
- time;
- fees; and
- client communication.

### 12.6 Trades and field users

**Lead concern:** completing work quickly with minimal administration.

**Message:**

Emphasise:

- mobile usability;
- simple task flows;
- time and attendance;
- site records;
- photos and evidence;
- drawings and documents;
- forms and inspections; and
- clear required actions.

### 12.7 Clients, developers and asset owners

**Lead concern:** visibility, control and information continuity.

**Message:**

> Keep project, supplier, commercial and asset information structured from delivery into operation, with controlled access to the records that matter.

Emphasise:

- project visibility;
- approvals;
- governance;
- asset information;
- lifecycle records;
- maintenance; and
- supplier performance.

### 12.8 Facilities and asset operators

**Lead concern:** reliable asset information and maintenance workflows.

**Message:**

> Connect asset records, maintenance activity, suppliers, costs and compliance history so operational information remains usable after construction is complete.

Emphasise:

- asset registers;
- maintenance;
- work orders;
- compliance;
- lifecycle records;
- suppliers; and
- cost history.

## 13. “NuBlox for…” proposition pattern

Audience pages should use the naming architecture defined in `02-brand-architecture-and-naming.md`.

Pattern:

> **NuBlox for [Audience]**

Then explain the relevant combination of shared products and capabilities.

Example:

### NuBlox for Quantity Surveyors

> Connect estimates, budgets, procurement, commitments, variations, valuations and forecasting with the wider project record.

The page should not imply that this is a separate codebase or independent product unless that is actually the case.

## 14. Product-family messaging pattern

Every customer-facing product should be described using the same structure.

### Product name

**NuBlox [Product]**

### One-line purpose

State what the product helps the customer control or complete.

### Problem

Explain the operational problem without exaggeration.

### Capability

Describe the important workflows and records.

### Connection

Explain which other NuBlox products or shared platform records connect to it.

### Outcome

Explain what improves for the customer.

Example:

**NuBlox Procurement**  
Manage supplier enquiries, purchasing and procurement activity in the same project and commercial context as estimates, commitments and delivery records.

## 15. Product naming in prose

Use the full product name on first meaningful reference:

> NuBlox Commercial provides project cost-control workflows.

Later references may use:

> Commercial

where the context is unambiguous.

Do not repeatedly write the complete NuBlox prefix in every sentence.

## 16. Product UX writing principles

The application interface is part of the brand.

### 16.1 Use verbs for actions

Prefer:

- Create quotation
- Submit valuation
- Approve order
- Assign action
- Record time

Avoid vague labels such as:

- Proceed
- Process
- Execute
- Action

where a more specific verb is available.

### 16.2 Name objects consistently

The same business object should not receive different names in different modules unless there is a deliberate terminology rule.

A controlled product glossary should eventually govern terms such as:

- organisation;
- contact;
- project;
- opportunity;
- estimate;
- quotation;
- supplier;
- purchase order;
- commitment;
- variation;
- valuation;
- asset;
- work order; and
- action.

### 16.3 Explain consequences

Confirmation or destructive actions should state what will happen.

Prefer:

> Delete this draft quotation? This removes the draft and its unsaved pricing lines.

Avoid:

> Are you sure?

### 16.4 Use status language deliberately

Statuses should communicate workflow state rather than emotion.

Prefer:

- Draft
- Submitted
- Under Review
- Approved
- Rejected
- Superseded
- Closed

### 16.5 Error messages should help recovery

Prefer:

> The purchase order could not be submitted because no supplier is selected. Select a supplier and try again.

Avoid:

> Error 400. Invalid request.

Technical detail may be available separately for support and diagnostics.

## 17. Tone by context

The NuBlox voice remains stable, but tone should adapt.

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Website | confident, clear, outcome-led |
| Sales proposal | commercially focused, specific, evidence-led |
| Product UI | concise, direct, task-oriented |
| Documentation | precise, structured, explanatory |
| Support | calm, diagnostic, solution-oriented |
| Security/compliance | factual, exact, non-promotional |
| Release notes | concise, technical where necessary, user-focused |
| Incident communication | direct, factual, transparent |
| Social content | accessible and energetic without becoming flippant |

## 18. Sentence and copy style

### Prefer

- active voice;
- short to medium-length sentences;
- concrete nouns;
- specific verbs;
- British English for UK-first communications;
- direct headlines;
- useful subheadings;
- measurable claims where evidence exists; and
- examples grounded in real work.

### Avoid

- inflated superlatives;
- unnecessary exclamation marks;
- excessive rhetorical questions;
- unexplained acronyms;
- long chains of adjectives;
- empty adjectives such as “cutting-edge”;
- technology buzzwords used as substitutes for outcomes; and
- pretending complexity does not exist.

## 19. Preferred vocabulary

Use terms such as:

- connected;
- structured;
- practical;
- shared;
- controlled;
- traceable;
- integrated;
- workflow;
- capability;
- project;
- commercial;
- operational;
- information;
- records;
- visibility;
- permissions;
- automation;
- integration; and
- platform.

Use “single source of truth” cautiously. It is often unrealistic where legitimate external systems remain authoritative for specific information.

Prefer language such as:

> shared authoritative record

or

> connected project record

where more accurate.

## 20. Language to avoid or qualify

Avoid or tightly qualify the following unless supported by evidence and context:

- revolutionary;
- game-changing;
- world-leading;
- best-in-class;
- seamless;
- frictionless;
- effortless;
- fully automated;
- eliminates all errors;
- guarantees compliance;
- complete ERP;
- complete accounting;
- AI-powered everything;
- digital transformation;
- one system replaces everything; and
- the only platform you will ever need.

These statements either overclaim, lack useful meaning or create expectations NuBlox may not be able to substantiate.

## 21. Technology language

Technology should normally support the story rather than lead it.

Customer-facing communications should not begin with:

- Svelte;
- MySQL;
- API architecture;
- database normalisation;
- bounded contexts; or
- implementation patterns.

Those details matter for technical evaluators and development documentation but are not the primary customer value proposition.

When discussing technology externally, connect it to customer value.

Example:

> NuBlox uses a structured relational data model so core business records can be reused across connected workflows rather than duplicated between isolated modules.

## 22. Automation and intelligent capability language

NuBlox should describe automation and intelligent assistance according to the actual function delivered.

Prefer:

- automate repeatable workflow steps;
- suggest likely actions;
- summarise project information;
- identify exceptions;
- assist review;
- extract structured information; or
- generate a draft for user review.

Avoid anthropomorphic or autonomous language unless the product genuinely behaves that way and appropriate controls exist.

Do not imply that automated output removes professional responsibility where judgement, statutory duty or contractual responsibility remains with a person or organisation.

## 23. Compliance and safety language

NuBlox may help users manage compliance information and workflows, but marketing should not claim that use of the software itself guarantees legal, contractual or regulatory compliance.

Prefer:

> Maintain structured inspection records and track corrective actions.

Avoid:

> Stay automatically compliant with every regulation.

## 24. Competitive language

NuBlox should differentiate through its own proposition rather than rely on attacks against competitors.

Prefer:

> Connect commercial, project and operational workflows through shared records.

Avoid:

> Unlike outdated competitors, NuBlox actually works.

Comparative claims should only be used where they are accurate, fair and supportable.

## 25. Calls to action

Calls to action should match the stage of the business and customer's intent.

## 29. Evidence and claim governance

All public claims should be classified internally as one of:

1. **Delivered capability** — available in the current product;
2. **Measured outcome** — supported by evidence;
3. **Design principle** — an established product or architecture commitment;
4. **Roadmap capability** — planned but not generally available; or
5. **Vision statement** — long-term intended direction.

Public copy should not blur these categories.

Examples:

- “NuBlox targets a normalised relational model” is a design commitment.
- “NuBlox reduces administration by 40%” would require measured evidence.
- “NuBlox Intelligence will identify commercial risk automatically” is a roadmap claim unless already delivered and validated.

This distinction is essential for credibility.

## 30. Terminology governance

NuBlox should eventually maintain a controlled terminology file or product glossary in the repository.

Each important term should define:

- canonical customer-facing name;
- internal/domain name where different;
- definition;
- permitted synonyms;
- terms to avoid;
- regional variants; and
- affected products/modules.

This will help keep database terminology, API language, UX labels, documentation and marketing aligned without forcing every technical identifier to become customer-facing copy.

## 31. British English baseline

UK-first communications should use British English by default.

Examples:

- organisation;
- authorised;
- standardise;
- licence as a noun;
- programme where referring to a programme of work.

Code identifiers, API schemas and external integration terminology may follow different technical conventions where necessary.

Future regionalisation should adapt terminology without changing the underlying master brand.

## 32. Verbal identity quality test

Before publishing NuBlox copy, ask:

1. Is the main point clear on first reading?
2. Does it describe a real customer problem or outcome?
3. Is the language specific enough to be meaningful?
4. Is every capability claim currently true or clearly framed as future direction?
5. Does the copy sound professional without sounding bureaucratic?
6. Would a built-environment professional recognise the work being described?
7. Have we avoided unnecessary technology or marketing jargon?
8. Does the message reinforce connection, control, practicality or structured information?
9. Does it fit the NuBlox branded-house architecture?
10. Can the claim be defended if challenged?

If not, rewrite it.
