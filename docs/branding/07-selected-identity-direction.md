# NuBlox Selected Identity Direction

**Document status:** Draft v1.0  
**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Scope:** Second-round refinement brief for the selected NuBlox master-brand identity  
**Parent documents:** `docs/branding/04-visual-identity-brief.md`, `docs/branding/05-logo-concept-directions.md`, `docs/branding/06-logo-concept-evaluation.md`

## 1. Purpose

This document converts the first-round logo selection into one coherent identity direction for refinement.

The selected route is not a finished logo. It is the controlled design hypothesis that should now govern second-round visual development.

## 2. Selected identity hypothesis

> **A proprietary modular `N` symbol paired with a precise custom `NuBlox` wordmark.**

The identity should communicate:

- modularity;
- connection;
- structure;
- digital capability;
- reliability;
- enterprise credibility; and
- long-term extensibility.

The symbol should relate naturally to the NuBlox name without becoming a literal stack of blocks or a construction-industry icon.

## 3. Primary symbol direction

The preferred symbol is a **modular `N`** built from a minimal number of controlled geometric components.

### Core construction principles

The final symbol should:

- fit comfortably inside a square bounding box;
- retain an immediately recognisable `N` silhouette;
- use a limited number of modular components;
- rely on geometry rather than decorative effects;
- maintain strong positive and negative space;
- remain legible at favicon size;
- work in a single flat colour;
- work reversed in white;
- avoid dependence on gradients, shadows or 3D rendering; and
- contain at least one proprietary construction detail that differentiates it from generic geometric `N` marks.

### Recommended geometric character

The preferred construction language is:

- vertical structural stems;
- a decisive diagonal connection;
- approximately 45-degree diagonal logic where optically appropriate;
- square or near-square modular proportions;
- controlled internal cuts or joins;
- no unnecessary curves; and
- optical corrections permitted where strict mathematical geometry appears visually unbalanced.

The symbol should feel **engineered rather than mechanical** and **modular rather than blocky**.

## 4. Three second-round symbol refinements

Three variants should be developed from the same underlying strategy.

### Variant A — Continuous Modular N

A simplified `N` using the fewest visible components and the strongest uninterrupted silhouette.

**Priority:** small-size clarity and longevity.

**Target behaviour:**

- best favicon performance;
- cleanest monochrome mark;
- minimal internal segmentation;
- strongest enterprise character.

### Variant B — Interlocking Modular N

An `N` formed by two or three visibly connected units.

**Priority:** communicate integration and modular capability.

**Target behaviour:**

- recognisable joins;
- clear sense of separate capabilities forming one system;
- no appearance of masonry, toy blocks or stacked cubes;
- joins remain visible without becoming fragile at small sizes.

### Variant C — Negative-space Modular N

A compact outer form in which part of the `N` is created through negative space or an internal cut.

**Priority:** greater proprietary recognition.

**Target behaviour:**

- memorable silhouette;
- excellent single-colour use;
- strong app-icon presence;
- enough distinction to justify formal similarity and trademark review.

## 5. Preferred refinement order

The second round should be judged in this order:

1. **Variant A — Continuous Modular N**
2. **Variant C — Negative-space Modular N**
3. **Variant B — Interlocking Modular N**

Variant A is currently the preferred design starting point because NuBlox must function as business software before it functions as a visual metaphor.

Variant C has the greatest potential to produce stronger proprietary ownership if its negative-space construction remains simple.

Variant B should only advance if the visible modular relationship adds meaning without reducing small-size performance.

## 6. Wordmark direction

The symbol should be paired with a restrained, highly legible custom wordmark:

> **NuBlox**

The wordmark should feel:

- precise;
- modern;
- confident;
- substantial without appearing heavy;
- technically credible; and
- suitable for sustained B2B use.

### Wordmark principles

The spelling must remain immediately readable.

The wordmark should preserve:

- capital `N`;
- lowercase `u`;
- capital `B`;
- lowercase `lox`.

Areas for controlled customisation include:

- the diagonal logic of `N`;
- the relationship between `u` and `B`;
- the bowl geometry of `B`;
- the proportions of `l` and `o`;
- a subtle proprietary treatment of `x`;
- kerning and overall rhythm; and
- alignment with the modular symbol geometry.

Avoid exaggerated cuts, stencil effects or futuristic letterforms that reduce readability.

## 7. Symbol and wordmark relationship

The symbol should not feel like an unrelated icon placed beside generic text.

The two parts should share visual logic through:

- stroke/weight relationship;
- diagonal angle;
- proportion;
- corner treatment;
- spacing; and
- overall visual density.

### Preferred primary lockup

```text
[Symbol]  NuBlox
```

The horizontal lockup should be the default corporate and product-header treatment.

A stacked lockup may be developed for square or narrow applications but should remain secondary.

## 8. Product endorsement system

The selected identity must support the branded-house architecture without creating separate logos for products.

Preferred structure:

```text
[Symbol] NuBlox
         Projects
```

or, where horizontal space permits:

```text
[Symbol] NuBlox | Projects
```

The exact separator and typographic hierarchy should be tested visually.

Product names remain descriptors rather than independent brands.

Required initial tests:

- NuBlox Projects;
- NuBlox Commercial;
- NuBlox Estimating;
- NuBlox Procurement;
- NuBlox Workforce; and
- NuBlox Assets.

## 9. Application icon and favicon

The standalone symbol is expected to become the primary application icon.

It must be tested at:

- 16 × 16;
- 24 × 24;
- 32 × 32;
- 48 × 48;
- 64 × 64;
- 128 × 128;
- 256 × 256; and
- 512 × 512 pixels.

### Small-size rule

If internal modular segmentation becomes indistinct below approximately 24 pixels, the smallest icon sizes may use a simplified approved micro-mark derived from the same geometry.

The favicon must not rely on fine gaps or colour gradients.

## 10. Initial colour shortlist

The identity should still be approved structurally in monochrome first.

For second-round application testing, the following colour territory is preferred as the lead route.

### Lead palette — Technical Blue

| Role | Working value | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| **NuBlox Ink** | `#07182E` | wordmark, dark surfaces, high-trust corporate use |
| **NuBlox Blue** | `#146EF5` | primary brand accent, interactive emphasis |
| **NuBlox Cyan** | `#20B8D8` | secondary accent, connection/modularity detail |
| **Cloud** | `#F7F9FC` | light application and website surfaces |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | reversed marks and primary light surface |

These values are **working concept colours**, not approved production tokens.

Initial contrast checks indicate that:

- NuBlox Ink on White provides very high contrast;
- NuBlox Blue on White is suitable for normal text at approximately WCAG AA contrast;
- NuBlox Cyan is better used against NuBlox Ink than as small text on white.

### Secondary colour routes still worth testing

- deep indigo instead of bright blue;
- blue/teal with reduced saturation;
- neutral-led identity using blue only as a proprietary accent.

The final palette must be selected from real application and brand tests rather than isolated colour swatches.

## 11. Gradient policy

Gradients may be explored as a **secondary digital expression**, but they must never define the core logo geometry.

The approved logo must always exist in:

- solid black;
- solid white;
- one approved brand colour; and
- flat-colour lockup form.

If a blue-to-cyan gradient is later adopted, it may be used selectively in:

- large application icons;
- motion;
- hero graphics;
- marketing imagery; and
- non-essential decorative brand moments.

It should not be required for recognition.

## 12. Typography strategy

Final typeface selection remains open, but the identity should use a contemporary sans-serif system suitable for both brand and dense product UI.

Selection criteria include:

- excellent screen rendering;
- strong numerals;
- tabular figures where required;
- clear distinction between similar characters;
- broad weight range;
- high legibility at 12–16 px UI sizes;
- licensing suitable for web/application distribution; and
- visual compatibility with the custom NuBlox wordmark.

The final wordmark may be custom-drawn even if the broader design system uses an existing type family.

## 13. UI application direction

The identity should enter the product quietly and consistently.

### Application shell

Preferred behaviour:

- symbol + `NuBlox` wordmark in the primary application shell;
- NuBlox Ink as the strongest dark-navigation candidate;
- Blue used for selected navigation, focus and key actions;
- Cyan used sparingly;
- neutral surfaces dominate dense business workflows;
- semantic colours remain separate from brand colours.

The interface should not become blue simply because blue is the brand colour.

## 14. Website application direction

The public website may use the identity more expressively than the product application.

Recommended visual relationship:

- strong NuBlox Ink/White contrast;
- generous white or near-white space;
- Blue as the dominant brand accent;
- modular `N` geometry used as a subtle framing or layout motif;
- structured diagrams rather than decorative cartoons;
- real built-environment photography where imagery is required.

## 15. Graphic-system extension

The modular symbol may provide a wider proprietary graphic language once its geometry is finalised.

Possible extensions include:

- modular grid fragments;
- connected paths;
- cropped portions of the `N` geometry;
- transitions between modules;
- workflow connectors;
- diagram framing; and
- motion based on components joining into a whole.

These extensions must remain subordinate to the logo and should not turn every brand application into a block pattern.

## 16. Monochrome approval gate

No symbol variant should progress to final identity status unless it succeeds in pure black and white.

The following tests are mandatory:

- black symbol on white;
- white symbol on black;
- black symbol at 16–32 px;
- laser/office-printer reproduction;
- low-resolution browser favicon simulation;
- single-colour document-header use; and
- simple embroidery/signage feasibility where relevant.

## 17. Similarity and ownability gate

Before final public adoption, the selected symbol and wordmark should be checked against relevant existing identities, particularly:

- B2B SaaS;
- construction technology;
- project-management software;
- ERP/business platforms;
- cloud/integration software; and
- brands using geometric `N` symbols.

The purpose is not only legal clearance. It is also to ensure NuBlox develops a visual asset that customers can actually recognise as its own.

## 18. Production asset requirements

Once final geometry is approved, production identity assets should include:

```text
brand/
├── logo/
│   ├── nublox-wordmark.svg
│   ├── nublox-symbol.svg
│   ├── nublox-lockup-horizontal.svg
│   ├── nublox-lockup-stacked.svg
│   ├── nublox-lockup-white.svg
│   └── nublox-lockup-black.svg
├── icons/
│   ├── favicon.svg
│   ├── favicon.ico
│   ├── icon-192.png
│   └── icon-512.png
└── products/
    ├── nublox-projects.svg
    ├── nublox-commercial.svg
    └── ...
```

SVG should remain the canonical vector format wherever practical.

## 19. Design-token relationship

Once colours and typography are approved, the visual identity should map directly into application design tokens rather than being recreated ad hoc in CSS.

Illustrative token groups:

```text
--brand-ink
--brand-primary
--brand-secondary
--surface-canvas
--surface-raised
--text-primary
--text-secondary
--border-default
--focus-ring
```

Semantic state tokens must remain independent from the brand palette.

## 20. Current decision

The NuBlox identity is now narrowed to one design system:

> **Modular N + precision NuBlox wordmark + restrained technical-blue visual system.**

This remains a **selected design direction**, not final logo approval.

## 21. Next visual deliverable

The next visual round should present three refined executions of this selected system:

1. Continuous Modular N;
2. Negative-space Modular N; and
3. Interlocking Modular N.

Each must be shown in the same comparison contexts:

- black and white;
- primary horizontal NuBlox lockup;
- app icon;
- favicon simulation;
- NuBlox Projects lockup;
- application header; and
- website header.

The objective of that round is to select the final symbol construction, not to reopen the broader logo strategy.
